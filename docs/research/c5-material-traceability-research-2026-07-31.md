## RG-5 — Material and product approval practice in AU civil

### The finding in one paragraph

AU civil runs **two stacked approval layers, not one**, and conflating them is the modelling trap. **Layer 1 is product/mix registration**, held by the *road authority* on a *central register*, valid across all of that authority's projects, carrying a registration number, a status and an expiry. **Layer 2 is project-level approval**, granted by the *superintendent-equivalent* for *that contract*, gated by a Hold Point, and it consumes Layer 1 as an input ("submit the mix design certificate which holds current registration"). The submitter at both layers is the **Contractor**, never the supplier directly. The approver at Layer 2 is the **superintendent-equivalent** — never the designer and never the RPEQ. And critically: **none of this comes from the contract's general conditions.** AS 4000-1997 contains no material-submittal regime at all. The approval register is a creature of the *technical specification*.

### What this does and does not settle

**Settled (grade A):** who submits (Contractor), who approves at project level (superintendent-equivalent — Administrator / Principal / Superintendent), what it is approved against (the technical specification clause plus currency on an authority register), what gates it (a Hold Point), the lead times, the difference between a *revision* and a *new* approval, and the fact that **both** per-supplier/per-product and per-project scopes exist simultaneously. RG-5 asked "per-project or per-supplier" as an either/or; **the answer is both, in a defined relationship**, and that is the finding.

**Not settled:** the C5 spec's RG-5 unblock condition asked for "a real project's approved-materials register (grade C)". **No such artefact was located publicly** — contractors' registers are internal documents. What was found instead is better for the *authority* half (the DTP status vocabulary at 5.13 is a real published register schema) and absent for the *contractor* half. **The column set of a head contractor's own approved-materials register remains unknown and needs a practitioner interview or one real register.**

## RG-6 — What a supplier conformance certificate / delivery docket must contain

### The finding in one paragraph

**Concrete has a governing standard; asphalt does not.** For concrete, **AS 1379 clause 1.7.3** defines a baseline "Identification Certificate" (the standard's own name for the delivery docket) and every authority spec examined *adds* to it rather than replacing it — so the honest model is *base list + per-authority extension*, not one list. For asphalt there is **no Australian Standard equivalent**: **AS/NZS 2891 is a family of test methods, not a supply or certification standard, and it specifies no docket contents at all** — the C5 spec's RG-6 row named it as a candidate source and that candidacy does not survive contact. The asphalt field list exists only at authority-specification level, and the three authorities disagree on whether one exists at all.

### What this settles

The C5 spec's RG-6 row warned: *"a field list inferred from one supplier's docket layout is grade C masquerading as A."* The trap is real but the shape of the answer is worse than that warning implies: **there is no single field list to extract.** There are four different authoritative lists (AS 1379 base, VIC concrete extension, NSW concrete list, VIC asphalt list), they overlap only partially, one material has no standard at all, and one jurisdiction declines to specify for that material. A `supplier_certificate` table with fixed columns would be wrong against at least two of the four on day one.

## RG-8 — Is there a formal quarantine state?

### The finding in one paragraph

**No. AU civil does not run a quarantine state for nonconforming material.** Across every authority document collected for this pass and every authority document already collected by earlier C-wave research passes — TfNSW Q6, B80, R53; TMR MRTS30, MRTS50, MRTS70; VicRoads Sections 610, 407, 204; Logan PSP5; WSA 05; and roughly 200 further specification and council documents in the shared corpus — the string `quarantin*` occurs **three times, all three being Red Imported Fire Ant biosecurity conditions in Toowoomba Regional Council development approvals**. Zero occurrences relate to material quality. What runs instead is a **nonconformance process whose enforcement mechanism is a prohibition on incorporation plus a Hold Point** — and material rejected *at delivery, before incorporation* generates no record with a state at all: it is simply refused and removed from site.

VIC: **"Asphalt delivered to the site, which is segregated, has been overheated, is too cold, contains separated binder, uncoated particles or which does not comply with the Specification shall be removed from the site."** | TMR **MRTS70**, July 2022, **cl 11.1** and Table 10.5; VicRoads/DTP **Section 407**, v18.0 Oct 2025, **cl 407.20** General | 31 Jul 2026 | **A** | Two jurisdictions, two materials, same answer: **removal, not retention in a state.** MRTS70 adds only that *"If a load is to be rejected due to differences between batches, a visual record should be kept"* — a **should**, and a photo, not a record type. |
| 8.15 | The nonconformance record is **lot-scoped**, not material-scoped. QLD lots exist expressly for, among other things, **"rejection of work"**, and the lot registration system must include **"the means for recording and/or cross-referencing records of compliance or Nonconformance related to the lot"** and **"a suitable method of identification for lots which replace nonconforming lots"**. NSW: maintain a register identifying every Lot. | TMR **MRTS50**, March 2025, **cl 7.1**, **cl 7.2(e),(f)**; TfNSW **Q6**, Ed 2 / Rev 0, **cl 3.9** | 31 Jul 2026 | **A** | **Directly validates CIVOS's existing lot-scoped `NCRLot`** against the C5 spec's stated worry that it might be the wrong grain. |

### What this settles

RG-8 asked whether contractors run a formal quarantine state **or** route it through NCR. The answer is **NCR, unambiguously, and the C5 spec's instinct to worry about duplicating the shipped NCR flow was correct**. Better than that: the reason there is no quarantine state is structural, not cultural. Quarantine is a *warehouse* concept — it presumes material sits in inventory under a status until released. AU civil delivery is **just-in-time to a lot**: concrete is placed within 75–90 minutes of batching, asphalt within a temperature window. There is no inventory to hold, so the control point is **"do not incorporate"**, enforced as a hold point, and the record is the NCR against the lot.

The one place a state *does* exist is the opposite end of the pipeline: **the authority's mix register** has real statuses (General / Conditional / Expired / Withdrawn, 5.13). Approval has a state machine; rejection does not.

## What this means for the C5.4 data model

Seven consequences, each traceable to a numbered claim above. These are findings, not a design — C5.4 remains blocked until RG-2 is also discharged, and the pilot gate in the C5 spec still applies to anything workflow-shaped.

**1. "Material approval" is two entities, not one.** A cross-project **product/mix registration** (authority-held, numbered, status-bearing, expiring) and a per-contract **approval/hold-point release** that *references* it. Modelling them as one table forces a false choice: registration data would be duplicated per project, or project approvals would be unable to differ. Evidence: 5.5, 5.6, 5.8, 5.10, 5.13, 5.15.

**2. If any approval status vocabulary is ever needed, one is already published — do not invent one.** `General · Conditional · Expired · Withdrawn` (5.13), with "current at the time of use" as the validity test and de-registration-to-Withdrawn as the revocation path (5.14). VIC concrete supplies the expiry semantics: valid 12 months from registration (5.10). This is exactly the kind of vocabulary the C5 spec's `[C5S-B1]` exists to stop CIVOS from guessing, and it did not have to be guessed.

**3. The approval→delivery join key is specified, in both materials and two jurisdictions.** The mix design's unique identifier is *required to appear on the delivery docket*: VIC concrete cl 610.07(b)(xiii) → cl 610.16(e)(v) (5.11, 6.7); VIC asphalt cl 407.20(b)(vii) "traceable to the mix registration number" (6.12); NSW carries nominated W/C and slump from trial mix onto the docket (6.10). **A delivery record that captures the mix/product identifier as transcribed text is doing real, specified work** — and it is the field that later makes a structured link possible without needing one now.

**4. The delivery↔test join key is also specified, and CIVOS has both ends already.** The docket serial number appears on test certificates expressly to provide an audit trail for analysing nonconforming concrete (6.3, grade B) — corroborated grade A by "Test reports shall be traceable to concrete delivery dockets" (6.4). C5.1's `batchRef` free-text field, which the C5 spec justified on honesty grounds alone, turns out to be the **specified** traceability token. That is a stronger justification than the one currently in the spec, and it is worth recording there.

**5. A fixed-column `supplier_certificate` table is refuted, not merely risky.** Four authoritative field lists exist for two materials and they are not supersets of one another (6.2, 6.7, 6.9, 6.12); asphalt has no standard at all (6.11); and one jurisdiction specifies no asphalt docket contents (6.14). Any fixed schema is wrong against at least two of them immediately. If structured extraction is ever built, it must be **per-authority, per-material profile-driven** — the shape C1 already uses for authority vocabularies — or it must stay a filed document with transcribed identifiers, which is what C5.1 already ships.

**6. Two docket properties break the "static supplier artefact" assumption.** The docket is **written on at the point of delivery** (water added on site must be recorded on it — 6.6), and it carries a **two-tier evidence split**: some fields printed on the docket, others merely "traceable to the batching plant and available on request" (6.8). A model treating the docket as an immutable supplier-issued PDF is wrong on the first count; a model assuming every specified field is *on* the artefact is wrong on the second.

**7. Do not build a quarantine state, and do not re-grain the NCR.** The prohibition-on-incorporation is a **hold point on a process**, not a status on a material (8.6, 8.8), and the nonconformance record is **lot-scoped by specification** (8.15) — which is what CIVOS already has. Rejected-at-delivery material generates **no record at all** in AU practice (8.14): it is removed from site, or under AS 4000 never delivered (8.13). The nearest thing to a modellable artefact is the NCR itself, and NSW publishes its four-field schema (8.4) should the shipped NCR ever need alignment. **The "rejected/quarantined material state" clause of program line 79 has no referent in AU civil practice** and should be considered for amendment on the same basis as the D2 clause the C5 spec already disposed of.

### Source access note

Several authority sites (`tmr.qld.gov.au` for some paths, `transport.nsw.gov.au`, `dli.nt.gov.au`, `ccaa.com.au`) return **HTTP 403** to automated fetchers but serve normally to a browser-identified `curl` request; `webapps.vicroads.vic.gov.au` **no longer resolves**, VicRoads specifications having moved under the Department of Transport and Planning. VicRoads Sections 610 and 407 were therefore read from **Wyndham City Council republications**, which carry the VicRoads/DTP copyright line and version stamp on every page (Section 610 — © VicRoads February 2020; Section 407 — v18.0, October 2025) and mark the council's own amendments inline with strikethrough. Where a council amendment was visible, the **VicRoads base text** is what is recorded above, not the council's variant. This is a **re-publication of a primary source with visible provenance**, which is why it is graded A; anyone re-verifying should prefer a direct DTP copy if one becomes reachable.

**One renumbering trap, recorded so the next reader does not fall into it.** The VIC asphalt delivery-docket clause is **407.20(b)** in the current v18.0 (October 2025). In earlier editions it was **407.16(b)** — that number still appears throughout Section 407's own 1992–93 amendment history, and 407.16 in the current edition is *Rate of Delivery*, an unrelated clause. A search that lands on the amendment log will cite a clause number that no longer means what it says. The same caution applies to AS 4000 (claim 5.16): the unamended 1997 standard puts Quality at **cl 29**, while a commonly-used amended form puts identical text at **cl 30**.

Every document read for this pass is a public specification. No purchased standard was accessed, and no clause number in this document was inferred rather than read — the two that were initially inferred from section headings (MRTS70's test-traceability clause and TfNSW Q6's lot-register clause) were re-opened and corrected to **cl 12.3** and **cl 3.9** before this document was committed.
